## cue 01
There's a one-time setup to do first. This is the settings screen. The extension uses a generative AI to draft search strategies, but it runs on your own AI subscription — a bring-your-own-key model. The key you register here is stored only in your browser and is never sent to the developer or any third party.

## cue 02
We'll use Google's Gemini. The "Get API key" link in the card opens Google AI Studio, where you can issue a key for free. Copy that key and paste it into this field.

## cue 03
The field is masked, so your key stays hidden even while screen sharing. What's being typed here is a dummy string for the demo.

## cue 04
When you hit Save, the extension automatically detects whether the key is on the free or paid tier. The result appears as a badge next to the card heading. If it's a free-tier key, the model switches automatically to one covered by the free quota, so you can start using it without worrying about your plan.

## cue 05
You can pick the model here. The default is Gemini 3.5 Flash. There's also an OpenRouter card just below — add a key there and you can use other models — but Gemini alone is plenty to get started.

## cue 06
The NCBI API key below is optional. Things work without it, but registering one makes PubMed queries faster. Once you've filled these in, press Save. That completes the setup.
